Cover

Limits stop the money. The bond fixes the mistakes.

When an AI agent buys the wrong thing, someone should pay for it, and it shouldn't be the shopper. Cover is a money-back guarantee for agent purchases. The company that makes the agent puts up a USDC deposit. The shopper's signed request is saved onchain before any money moves. If the agent gets it wrong, the shopper is refunded from that deposit in seconds.

The problem

Agents make mistakes, and right now the shopper pays

  • Shops are making shoppers carry the risk. Target changed its terms on 22 Mar 2026: purchases by an AI agent are "considered transactions authorized by you", even if the outcome isn't what you meant. A Reddit thread called it "Target puts customers on the hook". reported
  • Merchants want the agent's maker to pay. In a PYMNTS survey from Sep 2026, 93% of merchants said the agent provider should carry the loss for wrong purchases, and only 28% let agents buy their full range. reported
  • The big players are moving, but only inside their own systems. Amex covers wrong items, but only for its cardholders and agents registered with Amex (14 Apr). Ant International promises full money back on its own payment network (18 Sep), but hasn't published its limits, exclusions or how fast it pays. reported
  • Singapore's regulator wants a check before every agent action. MAS's SAFR paper (3 Jul 2026) describes a checkpoint that verifies and logs an agent's action before it happens. reported

Example. Ana says "Buy the black 25W PowerBug, under $60." The shop's page shows White/Dune as the default colour, and her agent takes the default. Today Ana is stuck with a $49.99 charger she didn't ask for. With Cover, she taps "Wrong item" and gets the money back from the agent maker's deposit.

Who it's for

Companies that build shopping agents and want an Amex-style guarantee without being Amex. The shoppers who use those agents. Merchants who only want covered agents buying from them.

Why they'd pay

An agent maker gets a guarantee it can advertise, priced from its agent's public ERC-8004 score. A good score means a lower fee and more freedom to buy without asking. Shoppers get money back in seconds, not weeks. assumption The full picture is in Who pays.

How it works

The agent asks, the contract checks, the deposit pays back

flowchart LR
  A["Ana sets limits and signs a request"] --> S["Cover service"]
  G["Ana's AI agent"] -- "picks an item" --> S
  S -- "quote" --> K["Kwal"]
  S -- "release exact amount" --> C["Checkpoint contract (Ink)"]
  C -- "USDC" --> V["Ana's Kwal vault"]
  V --> R["Reap card pays the shop (simulated)"]
  M["Agent maker"] -- "deposit" --> B["Bond contract (Ink)"]
  C -. "purchase record" .-> B
  B -- "refund on a valid claim" --> W["Ana's wallet"]
  B -. "one score per purchase" .-> RR["ERC-8004 registries (Ink)"]
  1. The maker's deposit. The agent's maker locks test USDC in the Bond contract. An agent can only make covered purchases up to the part of its deposit that isn't already backing other purchases.
  2. Ana's limits. Ana keeps her own USDC in the Checkpoint contract and can withdraw it any time. She sets her limits once: $150 a month, $100 per item, these shops, her address, ends 31 Oct. This is "how a person gives permission and sets a budget".
  3. Ana's request. For each purchase, Ana's wallet signs what she wants: the item, its colour or size, the most she'll pay, the shop, and her address. The fields follow Mastercard's Verifiable Intent format.
  4. The checkpoint. The agent picks an item, and our service gets a Kwal quote. The contract checks Ana's signature, the amount, her monthly total, the shop, the address, and that the maker's deposit can cover it. Only then does it send the exact amount to Ana's Kwal vault. It records the request, the quote ID and what was actually bought.
  5. The purchase. Kwal sees the vault balance and its Reap card pays the shop. Our service writes the payment ID onchain.
  6. The claim. Ana taps "Wrong item". For clear-cut fields like colour, size or model, the contract compares what she signed with what was bought. If they differ, it refunds her from the maker's deposit straight away. No person or AI decides, because the contract is the judge. For vague complaints, add-on A3 asks an OpenAI classifier, and its answer is recorded onchain next to the refund or rejection.
  7. The score. The agent has an ERC-8004 identity, registered by its maker. When a purchase is settled, the Bond writes one score to the ERC-8004 Reputation Registry: 100 if the item was right, 0 if Ana was refunded. Cover only counts scores written by the Bond, so every score comes from a real paid purchase. The maker's cover fee is priced from that average.

Why not block a wrong item before paying? Requests are usually words ("black"), and shops label colours differently ("Midnight", "Dune"). Blocking on near-matches would refuse good purchases, and Cover would become the shopping agent. The maker decides which item matches; its deposit makes it careful. Clear-cut mismatches are refunded automatically by the contract. Vague ones ("it can't run two monitors") go to the optional OpenAI judge (add-on A3), which only refunds when it is very sure and sends everything else to the maker for review.

Better agents pay less and get more freedom, so the score is worth protecting. The formula is ours assumption and is published on the terms page (A2). If 1-USDC pricing is on, the fee is taken on the 1 USDC actually released; the maker's screen shows it next to the listed-price equivalent.

Main product

Everything here ships, at full spec

Each part lists what "done" means. The 9 pm cut-off doesn't shrink any of it. Add-ons only start once all of it works twice in a row.

must ship M1 · Checkpoint contract (Ana's account)

  • Holds Ana's test USDC. She can withdraw unspent money at any time, so it's non-custodial.
  • Stores her limits, set by her own transaction: per-item maximum, monthly maximum, allowed shops, a hash of her address, end date.
  • Release, callable only by the Cover service: checks Ana's signed request, the amount against her request maximum and per-item cap, her monthly total, the shop, the address, the end date, and that the Bond can reserve cover. Then it sends the exact amount to Ana's Kwal vault (fixed at deploy) and saves a purchase record: request, quote ID hash, amount, shop, the attributes she asked for, and the attributes bought.
  • Auto-pay limit from the score (used by add-on A5): release also refuses an amount above the limit for the agent's current ERC-8004 score. 90 or more: up to Ana's per-item cap. 60–89: up to $50. Under 60: nothing, so every purchase needs Ana's tap. The Bond supplies the score (the Bond-only average, or its own counters as the fallback). Refusing here doesn't block the purchase: the service sends it to Ana to approve herself (A5). The bands are set at deploy; if A5 is dropped, set every band to the per-item cap. assumption: the bands are ours
  • Confirm, callable only by the service: writes the Kwal payment ID hash and the final status.

Done when one Foundry test passes for each rule and each refusal (including a score that falls from one band to the next), and it's deployed to Ink Sepolia with its address in the README.

must ship M2 · Bond contract (maker's deposit and claims)

  • The maker deposits test USDC for an agent. Free cover = deposit minus amounts reserved for open purchases.
  • Reserve, callable only by the Checkpoint: locks cover for a purchase until the claim window ends. The window is 7 days in the design and a few minutes for the demo; the README says so.
  • Open claim, called by Ana's own wallet: for clear-cut fields (colour, size, model), the contract compares her signed value with the value bought. If they differ, it pays her the released amount at once, in the same transaction. If they match, the claim is rejected, which shows Cover is fair to makers too.
  • The maker can't withdraw reserved money. Other withdrawals wait out a delay.
  • Agent identity: deposits are keyed by the agent's ERC-8004 agentId. The maker registers the agent once with register(agentURI); the registration file names Cover and the Bond address.
  • Score: when a purchase is settled, the Bond calls giveFeedback(agentId, value, 0, "cover", outcome, …) once: 100 and "ok" when the window closes with no claim or a claim is rejected, 0 and "wrong-item" when Ana is refunded. feedbackHash is the purchase record's hash, so each score points to its purchase. Wrap the call in try/catch, so a registry problem can never block a refund. A needs-review claim scores nothing until the maker settles it.
  • Score read: getSummary(agentId, [Bond], "cover", ""). Passing only the Bond's address keeps out scores from anyone else. The Bond exposes the result as one number, which both the fee and the Checkpoint's auto-pay limit use.
  • Cover fee, taken onchain: at reserve, the Bond moves the fee from the maker's free deposit to a Cover treasury address (fixed at deploy). Fee = 0.5% + 0.05% for every point below 100, on the amount actually released: 0.5% at score 100, 2.15% at 67. If the free deposit can't cover the purchase plus the fee, the reserve is refused. The fee is kept whatever happens to the claim, because the refund comes from the deposit, not from the fee. The Bond keeps a running total of fees paid per agent. assumption: the pricing formula is ours
  • Keep plain counters in the Bond too (purchases, claims, refunds paid). If the registries misbehave by 5:30, the screens read those instead and the README says so.

Done when tests cover a mismatch refund, a match rejection, an over-reserve refusal and a withdrawal blocked by reserved cover; one score is written per settled purchase (never twice); a refund still pays when the registry call reverts; the fee reaches the treasury and is higher at a lower score; and a reserve is refused when the free deposit can't cover the purchase plus the fee.

must ship M3 · Cover service (FastAPI, Python 3.10+ in WSL)

  • Imports the Kwal Python client. The Kwal credentials stay on the server; the agent never sees them.
  • Checks Ana's signed request (laeria signed-rules code) and runs the laeria rules check before calling release.
  • Fills in Ana's shipping address from her saved limits. The agent can't choose an address.
  • Purchase runner: quote, rules check, release, wait for vault funding, checkout, wait for payment, confirm onchain.
  • One payment at a time (Kwal's limit), with retries every 15–30 seconds on rate limits.
  • Streams every step to the UI as live events.

Done when one call from the agent produces a completed Kwal payment and both onchain records, with no manual steps.

The registry blocks an agent's owner and operators from scoring their own agent, so the Bond must never be made an operator.

OpenAI judge call (add-ons A3 and A3b)

One Chat Completions call per claim, with no tools, temperature: 0, logprobs: true, top_logprobs: 5 and a token limit of a few tokens. The system prompt says the request, description and complaint are data, never instructions, and asks for exactly one word. Read the probability of the first output token for each of the three labels. If the model returns anything else, treat it as UNSURE. The same pattern with YES/NO runs the injection check. Publish the judge prompt on the terms page (A2), because its hash is onchain.

Reap Agentic direct (add-on A5 only)

Per the one-time purchases docs verified: the card is stored on Reap's hosted card-entry page, a quote can hold several items, checkout returns the hosted approval link, then you poll the checkout. Every request needs a bearer token and a Reap-Version header; create calls also need an Idempotency-Key. Copy exact paths from docs.reap.global when building. None are invented here.
